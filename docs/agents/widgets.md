# The widget kit of the iced app

`app/src/ui/widgets/` is what every page is built from. It knows no Host and no container. If a page needs a control or an overlay that isn't here, add it here: a page never writes its own.

Look at all of it with `cargo run -p homeport-app --example widgets --features gallery` (themes, English and Arabic, everything pressable). Its tests draw it to `app/tests/snapshots/widgets-*.png` and drive it with the keyboard (`app/src/ui/widgets/gallery/tests.rs`).

The theme has its own, `cargo run -p homeport-app --example gallery --features gallery`, held to `app/tests/snapshots/theme-*.png` in both themes and both directions. Both galleries are built for tests and with the `gallery` feature only: their English literals are not in the app.

- **Icons** take the colour of the text around them (`theme::icon`), as Lucide's `currentColor`: give one a style only where it differs from that text.
- **Weights.** Use `theme::font::medium()`, `semibold()` and `bold()` for text in the app's language, the constants only for text that is Latin whatever the language: Arabic, Hindi, Japanese, Korean, Chinese and Russian are drawn by the OS's fonts, which have Regular and Bold.

## The data table

`widgets::table` is `DataTable` and the Board's virtual list in one: a table over any rows that builds only those in view (and four more on each side), so 100,000 rows cost what 20 do. Look at it on the gallery's _Table_ page; `table/mod.rs` has the example to copy, `ui/features/app/hosts` a page that uses it.

- **The page keeps a `table::State<Key>`** (made once, in `open`: `State::new().selecting(Mode::Multi).sorted(Sort::by("name", Order::Ascending))`) and writes two functions that the view and `update` both call: `columns()` and `rows(..) -> table::Rows` (`Rows::new(&list, |row| row.id.clone())`). `Table::new(&state, columns(), rows, Message::Table).view()` draws it; `state.update(message, &rows, &columns())` takes every `table::Message` and gives back `Event::Opened(key)`, `Event::Selected` or `Event::Arranged`. `update` needs the rows and the columns because the keyboard goes through the rows in the order they show: it works that order out again if the list changed since the view. A page's state is one variant among fifty: box the table's (`Box<table::State<_>>`).
- **Columns are data**: `Column::new(id, t!(..), |row| cell)`, then `.width(Width::Fill(2))` (a share; `Width::Fixed` for pixels), `.between(min, max)`, `.align(Align::End)` for numbers, `.sort_by(|a, b| ..)` to make the header a button, `.descending_first()` for numbers, `.typed(|row| row.name.clone())` for the column typing looks in, `.hideable(false)` for the one that stays. A cell is any element, cut where its column ends; `table::text` is a line of `text-sm` that never wraps. Sort text with `table::sort::natural`: whatever the case, digits by their value (`host-2` before `host-10`), as TanStack's automatic sort; sort by the value, never by its translated label.
- **Rows are known by their key**, never by their place: the selection, the line the keyboard is on, an open menu and the scroll position survive a list that is read again, sorted or filtered, and rows added or removed above what shows don't move it. A row that left the list is let go of by the table (deselected, its menu closed); a selected row the search only hides stays selected.
- **Searching** is done as the query is typed, whatever the case unless `Aa` is on, as text unless `.*` makes it a regular expression (the `regex` crate's: linear time, no look-around). Matches are marked and counted ("3 of 17"), Enter in the field or F3 goes to the next and Shift with either to the one before, round at the ends, bringing the match into view (which pauses the view). `Filter` shows only the lines that match, and what the viewer says itself ("1,234 lines skipped", "end of logs"). Nothing of it waits in `update`: plain text of up to 256 bytes is made into its matcher there, anything else (a regular expression, whose cost is its author's) by a task, within 4,096 bytes and 2 MiB; up to 2,048 unread lines of up to 512 KiB together are read in `update`, more are read 65,536 at a time by a task on a blocking thread, so typing a letter over a million lines returns at once and the count grows as slices come back. `state.search_error()` is an `AppError`: a query that can't be read (`ui.log.invalidRegex`, the crate's words as its detail), which marks the field and is said beside it, or a search whose task died twice on the same lines (`ui.log.searchFailed`); a slice is read again once before that, and no line is ever counted as read that was not.
- **Groups** are `Rows::group_by(|row| row.stack.clone())`: the rows of a group under a header that closes and opens, the groups by name and before the rows of none, as the containers of a Stack. `Table::group_header(|name, rows| GroupHeader { label, cells })` says what the header shows beside its chevron and under which columns (a Stack's totals). The header's checkbox selects its rows.
- **Sorting, filtering and a selection are checked on every view**, a pass over the list (0.7 to 1.3 ms for 20,000 sorted rows, 3 to 4 ms for 100,000). A page that knows when its rows change says so with `Rows::revision(n)` and pays nothing in between; it must then change `n` when the rows or the search text do.
- **Text that doesn't fit** is `basic::truncated(text)`, which ends with an ellipsis instead of being cut through a letter. `.lines(2)` lets it wrap onto a second line before anything is cut (a row that holds a name over an id has the room), and `.with_tip()` shows the whole text under the pointer while it is cut, and only then: a cut text always has one. On a `Stage`, `frame.fitted()` gives what each such text says and what it shows, for a test that holds a page to showing a text whole. A status with a health badge is `ui/domain/containers::cells::status`: the badge under the status, not beside it. Beside a badge or a mark, put it in a container that fills and tell the row to fill (_Rules for a page_). `table::width_for(sample)` is the least width for a column as long as the language makes it (a size with its unit).
- **Always sorted.** `State::always_sorted()` makes a header go from one way to the other and never to unsorted: a table React sorted by one column at all times (the Board).
- **Selecting.** `Mode::Multi` adds the column of checkboxes. A press selects the row, Ctrl or Cmd adds or removes one, Shift selects from the last one; the checkbox over the rows selects those that show. With `State::opening_on_press()` a plain press opens the row instead, as `onRowClick` did. `state.mark(Some(key))` is `selectedId`: one row drawn as selected and scrolled to once it is in the list. `.selection_bar(Some(buttons))` is a line over the table for what can be done to the selected rows; pass `None` when nothing is selected, never leave the call out (its room stays, and so does the tree). The room is kept on purpose: a table whose press selects would otherwise move its rows under the pointer between the two presses that open one. A page that has a line of filters or a header puts the buttons there instead, as the React pages did, and no band stands over its table (`ui/domain/containers::actions::view::selection_bar` in the Containers page's filters and the Board's header).
- **Menus.** `.row_menu(|row| items)` puts a `…` button at the end of each row; the secondary button on a row, the menu key and Shift+F10 open it too. The items are asked only for the row whose menu is open.
- **Headers** that don't sort are cut where their column ends, with an ellipsis at the end the language reads to and the whole name under the pointer; one that sorts keeps its arrow beside a whole name.
- **Columns.** The end of a header is dragged to resize its column, no further than what the others' least widths leave; two presses there give it its own width back. `table::column_items(&state, &columns(), Message::Table)` are the items of a menu that hides and shows them. Columns whose least widths don't fit scroll sideways (Shift with the wheel, the arrows across, the scrollbar under the rows): nothing is pinned.
- **No rows.** `.loading(resource.is_pending())` shows skeletons under the columns, `.failed(Some(error_message(..)))` the failure in place of the rows, `.empty(empty_state(..).view())` or `.empty_label(t!(..))` what an empty list says ("Nothing here." unless told).
- **Height.** The table is as tall as its rows up to `.max_height(600.0)`, then scrolls: a page is in a scroll area, which gives all the height asked for. `.fill()` takes the height a pane gives instead.
- **What a page keeps**: `state.sort().keys()`, `state.widths()` and `state.hidden()` are plain data, to store when `Event::Arranged` comes and to give back with `State::sorted`, `with_widths` and `with_hidden`. The table stores nothing itself.
- **Accessibility**: nothing is wired. The hooks wait on the screen-reader spike (#228), which has not run; `ui.table.selectAll` and `selectRow` are in the catalogue and unused until then.

The keyboard, while the rows have focus (`table/grid/input.rs`):

| Key                     | What it does                                         |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Up, Down                | the line before, the line after, scrolled into view  |
| Page Up, Page Down      | a screen of lines                                    |
| Home, End               | the first line, the last                             |
| Shift with any of them  | selects from where the selection started to that row |
| Space                   | selects or deselects the row                         |
| Enter                   | opens the row                                        |
| Space, Enter on a group | closes or opens the group                            |
| Left, Right on a group  | close it, open it (the other way round in Arabic)    |
| Left, Right elsewhere   | scroll the columns that don't fit                    |
| Ctrl or Cmd + A         | selects every row that shows                         |
| Escape                  | deselects everything                                 |
| Menu, Shift + F10       | opens the row's menu; Escape there gives focus back  |
| Letters                 | go to the next row that starts with them (`typed`)   |
| Tab                     | leaves the rows                                      |

Tab goes through the checkbox over the rows, each header that sorts (Enter and Space sort, Shift adds the column to the sort), then the rows as one stop. The ring shows on the line the keyboard is on, for a key of the rows (not for Ctrl or Shift held for a press), and nowhere while that line is scrolled out of view. A table with a `typed` column takes the letters while its rows have focus: a page's one-letter shortcuts don't fire then.

What a view costs is held two ways (`table/tests/`). `counts.rs` runs with every `cargo test` and counts, for 100,000 rows, the cells built (a screen of them) and the rows compared, filtered and asked for their key (none, or one pass where the list says so): numbers no machine changes. `perf.rs` times a frame (`cargo test -p homeport-app --lib table::tests::perf -- --ignored --nocapture --test-threads=1`) and fails when the table's own share of a scroll step, or the `view` of a changed row, at 20,000 rows of 8 columns goes over the ticket's 2 ms (eight times that in a build that is not optimised; `TABLE_PERF_OPTIMISED=1` with the `--config` flags its rustdoc gives holds it to 2 ms). On 4 cores of a cloud VM, a 1100 × 720 window, software renderer, optimised, two runs:

| Rows               | A scroll step: `view` + layout + draw                  | Software renderer, the whole window |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------- |
| 100, 5 columns     | 0.3 ms                                                 | 16 ms                               |
| 100,000, 5 columns | 0.3 to 0.4 ms (sorted: 3 to 4, or 0.3 with a revision) | 16 ms                               |
| 20,000, 8 columns  | 0.8 to 1.1 ms sorted (0.3 with a revision)             | 15 to 16 ms                         |

A row that changed costs 0.65 to 0.72 ms of `view` at 20,000 rows. The pixels are the software renderer's, the same for 100 rows as for 100,000, and by themselves about a frame at 60 a second on that machine; the window draws with wgpu, which was not measured.

## The log viewer

`widgets::log` is `LogViewer`: the lines of one or several sources as they come, followed, searched, selected and saved. It is not a terminal: it keeps lines, not a screen. Look at it on the gallery's _Log_ page (a sample, a million lines, or a stream of 50,000 lines a second); `log/mod.rs` has the example to copy.

- **The page keeps a `log::State`**, made once in `open` with how many lines to keep: `log::State::new(cap)` (the cap is a setting upstream; `.wrapping(false)`, `.with_timestamps(true)` and `.with_sources(labels)` say how it starts). `log::Viewer::new(&state, Message::Log).toolbar(controls).failed(failure).height(..).view()` draws it: `toolbar` is what the page adds before the viewer's own controls (which container, how far back, stdout and stderr apart), `failed` an `error_message` shown over the lines when the stream broke. It takes the height it is given; in a page that scrolls, give it one (`.height(420.0)`).
- **`state.update(message)` takes every `log::Message`** and returns `(Task<log::Message>, Option<log::Event>)`. Map the task to the page's message and return it: it is how a search reads a large buffer, how a regular expression is built, how a large selection is copied, and how Ctrl + F reaches the search field. Act on the event: `Event::Copy(text)` is for `ctx.copy`; `Event::Download(lines)` is what to save (below); `Event::Timestamps(on)` tells a page whose stream is asked for timestamps only while they show to open it again, as the React viewer did; `Event::Cleared` says the Clear button was pressed.
- **A stream feeds it** through the page's `subscription` (`streams::subscription(.., shapes::logs)`, or `shapes::lines` over another `*_subscribe`). For each `Update::Batch { items, dropped }`: `let batch = shapes::log_lines(items)`, then `state.skipped(dropped)`, `state.extend(batch.lines.into_iter().map(log::Line::from))` (return its task, mapped), `state.end()` if `batch.ended`, and keep `batch.error` as a `Surfaced` for `.failed(..)`. Batches come at most 60 times a second, so a stream of 50,000 lines a second is 60 calls of about 833 lines; `extend` looks at the new lines only. `Update::Failed` is a failure too; `Update::Ended` needs nothing more. When the page opens the stream again (other options, another container), `state.reset()` first: no line, no source and nothing a program left open (a colour, half a sequence) is kept, as React reset its terminal; the query and how the lines show stay. `state.set_sources(labels)` then gives the new stream's sources their order. `state.clear()` is the Clear button's: the stream goes on, so its sources keep their colours.
- **Timestamps.** A line of a stream that was asked for timestamps says so: `log::Line::from(line).timestamped(true)`. The Timestamps toggle then shows and hides them without the stream being opened again, so ask the Engine for timestamps from the start (`LogOptions::timestamps`) and leave them hidden (`with_timestamps(false)`). The timestamp is what stands before the first space when it starts with a digit (up to 64 printable ASCII bytes); it is taken off before the program's text is read, so nothing the program writes reaches it (a carriage return starts the message over, not the line), and a line that is nothing but its timestamp has an empty message.
- **Several sources** (`Line::source`, which `From<LogLine>` fills): each line starts with its source's label, padded to the longest and in one of six colours, in the order the sources were first seen; `with_sources` gives that order before any line came. Each source has its own terminal state, so a colour one of them left open does not run into the others' lines.
- **Following.** The view stays on the latest line. A scroll up (the wheel, a key, the thumb) pauses it: it then stays on its line whatever comes after and whatever is dropped before, until that line is itself dropped (it is then on the oldest line kept). It follows again when scrolled back to the end, with End, the Follow toggle, or "Jump to latest", which shows while the view is paused away from the latest line, in room of its own under the rows (two rows' worth, inside the frame, at the end of the row in either direction): it covers no line. Paused on the latest line (the Follow toggle turned off there) nothing shows, and a notch of the wheel down follows again.
- **Colours.** SGR sequences are read (the 16 colours and their bright forms, the 256, 24-bit colours with `;` or `:`, bold, dim, italic, underline, inverse, strike, and every reset); every other escape sequence is taken out and never drawn; a sequence cut by the end of a line is finished by the start of the next. The 16 are the theme's terminal palette; any colour that would not read on what is behind it (3:1) is moved towards the text's colour until it does, so both themes stay readable. Bold is the bright form of the first eight colours and nothing else: the monospaced face has one weight, so bold text in the default colour or in any other colour draws as it would without (the React terminal did the same: `fontWeightBold: 'normal'`). A code no terminal knows changes nothing; the colour of an underline (`58`) is read and not drawn. A tab goes to the next multiple of eight cells of the message, and a carriage return starts the message over (a progress bar keeps its last state).
- **Wrapping.** A line longer than the viewer goes on the next row at the viewer's last cell, as in a terminal (not at a word); the Wrap toggle turns that off, and long lines then scroll sideways (Shift with the wheel, Left and Right, the thumb under the rows). A wrapped line is as many rows as its cells divided by the viewer's (a character of two cells that would straddle a row's last cell starts the next row, so a line that holds one has its row starts listed instead): the font is monospaced, so no line is ever measured, and the view is anchored to a line and a row of it, never to a distance in pixels. Whatever the width, only the lines in view are looked at, and a row that stays in view is not shaped again.
- **Searching** is done as the query is typed, whatever the case unless "Aa" is on, as text unless ".\*" makes it a regular expression (the `regex` crate's: linear time, no look-around). Matches are marked and counted ("3 of 17"), Enter in the field or F3 goes to the next and Shift with either to the one before, round at the ends, bringing the match into view (which pauses the view). A regular expression that can't be read marks the field and says so; nothing is searched until it is mended. `Filter` shows only the lines that match. Up to 2,048 unread lines are read in `update`; more are read 65,536 at a time by a task on a blocking thread, so typing a letter over a million lines returns at once and the count grows as slices come back.
- **Selecting** is by text, as in a terminal: a drag, a press with Shift from where the selection started, two presses for a word, three for a line, Ctrl or Cmd + A for everything. Ctrl or Cmd + C and the Copy button give `Event::Copy` with what shows (labels and timestamps included, colours not), a wrapped line as one line. A selection over more than 4,096 lines or 1 MiB is read by a task. A drag past the rows takes the view and the selection a row further each time the pointer moves.
- **Saving.** `Event::Download(lines)` carries every line kept, filtered or not, as they were when the button was pressed (`lines.lines()`, `lines.write_to(file)`, `lines.len()`): `source | text` without colours, with timestamps while they show. It is cheap to hold and can be written on another thread, which is what a page does with it: the save dialog (`ctx.platform.save_file`, whose answer is a path, a cancel or a failure to say), then `platform::files::save` with `lines.write_counted(out, |written| meter.at(..))`, which tells how far a long save is (`docs/agents/iced-migration.md`, _The platform layer_; `gallery/log.rs` is the example, a million lines included).
- **In Arabic** the controls, the scrollbar and "Jump to latest" mirror; the log's text starts on the left and Left and Right scroll it the same way.
- **Accessibility**: nothing is wired, as for the table (#228).

The keyboard, while the rows have focus (`log/pane/input.rs`); they are one Tab stop, after the controls:

| Key                | What it does                                     |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Up, Down           | a row up, a row down                             |
| Page Up, Page Down | a screen of rows                                 |
| Home               | the oldest line                                  |
| End                | the latest line, which is followed again         |
| Left, Right        | scroll long lines sideways, when they don't wrap |
| Ctrl or Cmd + A    | selects every line that shows                    |
| Ctrl or Cmd + C    | copies the selection                             |
| Ctrl or Cmd + F    | goes to the search field                         |
| F3, Shift + F3     | the next match, the one before                   |
| Escape             | deselects                                        |

`/` on a page is `form::focus_search(state.search_id())`.

Its limits:

- **Memory** is the text, sixteen bytes a line, sixteen a coloured run and sixteen a line that matches the search: about 130 MB for a million lines of a hundred bytes. The cap is the only bound; nothing is written to disk.
- **Cells are counted, not measured**, by Unicode's tables (`unicode-width`, which was already in the tree): two for East Asian wide characters and emoji, none for what combines (accents, Arabic and Indic signs, joiners; what a joiner joins is part of the character before), one for the rest. Text the monospaced face draws (Latin, Cyrillic, Greek, box drawing) is exact. Each run of another script is placed at its own cell, and so is the text after it, but within the run the OS's font decides: its glyphs may be narrower or wider than their cells, so a mark of the search or a selection's edge inside such a run can be off by part of a character, and a run that reads right to left is laid out by the shaper while a selection across it follows cells.
- **A line of megabytes** (minified JSON) is walked once when it first shows: it gets marks every 128 cells, the starts of its rows if a wide character keeps them from being a division, and the list of its matches (for 64 such lines at a time). A layout then walks what it shows (`log/tests/layout.rs` counts it). Building that list is a search of the whole line, once per query.
- **A batch of 50,000 lines at once** (the stream's whole backlog, when the window was behind) is one `extend` of 15 ms: a frame is late once. A batch larger than the cap is trimmed as it goes, never held whole.
- **The selection has no caret**: the keyboard selects everything or nothing. A drag past the rows scrolls only while the pointer moves.
- **The scrollbar counts lines**, not rows: among wrapped lines of very different lengths the thumb moves unevenly.
- **Sources**: 65,534 at most; the widest line counted for the sideways scroll is never forgotten until the lines are cleared.

## The time-series chart

`widgets::chart` is `TimeChart`: lines, or areas, of live values over a time axis that moves with them. Look at it on the gallery's _Chart_ page (one series, an area, two counters with a restart, a stream with holes, ten series of an hour, and a sample a second if asked); `chart/mod.rs` has the example to copy.

- **The page keeps a `chart::State` per chart**, made once in `open`: `chart::State::new(series, cap)` is how many series it has and how many samples it keeps (`STATS_WINDOW`, 300). `.counter(index)` says a series is a counter (bytes received): it is drawn as its rate per second. `.window(seconds)` makes the time axis show that long before the newest sample and lets go of what is older; without it the axis spans the samples kept, as React's did. `.breaking_after(seconds)` stops the line where the stream stopped for longer than that (uPlot joined across).
- **A stream feeds it**: `state.push(time, &[Some(value), ..])` for each sample, the time in seconds since 1970 and one value per series in the series' order. Appending moves no other sample (rings: `samples.rs`). `state.reset()` before the page follows another container: no sample and no counter's last reading is kept, so the new stream's first rate is not taken from the old one's count; which series the legend hides stays. `state.latest(series)` is the figure a card's title shows beside its name.
- **The view**: `Chart::new(&state, vec![Series::new(t!(..)), ..], Message::Chart).unit(Unit::Bytes).view()`. The labels are read at each draw, so the state keeps no text. `.height(160.0)` is the plot's (React's default), `.without_legend()` leaves the legend out, `.zone(tz)` says the times on another clock than this computer's (the gallery and the snapshots use UTC). `state.update(message)` takes the chart's one message, `chart::Message::Toggle(index)`, the legend's.
- **Units.** `Unit::Number`, `Percent`, `Bytes`, `BytesPerSecond`, `BitsPerSecond` and `Custom(fn(f64) -> String)` (cores and millicores) say the axis and the tooltip through `i18n::format`: `format::rate` (`format.perSecond`) and `format::bit_rate` (`format.bits.*`, powers of 1000) are new with the chart. The y axis goes from zero to a tenth over the highest value in view (uPlot's range, as React asked for it), to 1 when nothing is above zero, and under zero the same way if a value is; its ticks stand on 1, 2 or 5 times a power of ten, or for sizes on a power of two of the unit they are said in (128 MB, 256 MB).
- **What a stream may send** is settled when it is pushed: a value that is missing, `NaN` or infinite is a gap, where the line stops and starts again (a sample alone between two gaps is a dot); a counter that went down (the container restarted) has a gap, never a negative rate, and one that missed a reading takes its next rate from the last reading it had; a sample whose time is not after the newest is left out and the rest is kept, as React's `rates` left out one point; a time that is no number is left out. Values that are all equal are a flat line under the top of a range that still has a height.
- **Colours and dashes.** docs/BRAND.md keeps charts neutral: a page that ports a React chart says `Series::new(..).tone(Tone::Ink)` and `.tone(Tone::Grey).dashed()` (`--chart-1` to `-3` are ink, `-4` and `-5` grey). A series that names nothing gets `Look::of(index)`: ink, a blue, an olive and grey (`Tokens::series` holds the two hues, which are the iced app's own), with five dash patterns, so no two of twenty series share both and colour is never the only cue; the legend and the tooltip show each line as it is drawn beside its name. `chart/tests/palette.rs` holds each colour to 3:1 on the page and on a card in both themes, each pair to staying apart for eyes without red, green or blue cones (Machado's matrices, 0.115 in Oklab), none to being near Signal red or amber, and the measure itself to catching the terminal's red and green. `.filled()` tints what is under the line.
- **The pointer** on the plot shows a crosshair on the nearest sample in view (never the one before a window), a mark on each line there and a tooltip beside it: the time, then each series that shows with its value, a missing one said as such. It stands after the crosshair the way the language reads, before it when that side lacks room, and always inside the plot, so what the axes say is never under it; more rows than the plot is tall go in columns, and the series that still have no room are counted in a last row (`+12`, `ui.chart.more`), which the legend names. A plot too small for the time and one row has the crosshair alone. A name too long for it is cut with an ellipsis there; the legend has it whole.
- **The legend** is under the plot, start to end, wrapping. Each entry is a button (Tab reaches each in turn; Enter, Space or a press hides its series and shows it again) with a tooltip that says so and names the series (`ui.chart.hide`, `ui.chart.show`). A hidden series keeps its entry, muted and without its line, and no longer counts in the range.
- **In Arabic** the y axis is on the right, where text starts, the legend and the tooltip mirror, and the axis is said in the language's words. Time still runs left to right, as on the React chart: `TimeChart.tsx` set no direction on uPlot's scales, and a canvas does not mirror (read from the source, not checked in the running Tauri app).
- **Display density.** The canvas is drawn in the window's pixels from logical sizes, so lines are sharp at any scale; hairlines stand on pixel centres. The snapshots draw at twice the size.

Its limits:

- **A point a pixel.** A series with more samples in view than the plot has pixels is thinned with Largest-Triangle-Three-Buckets, which keeps a peak of one sample (`chart/tests/thinning.rs`). The buckets stand on time, about a pixel of it on a round number of seconds, not on how many samples came before: a full ring that drops a sample for each new one keeps the same points of what stays, so the line does not shimmer. Between gaps, a series draws a run a pixel at the most: one whose every other sample is missing is a dot a pixel, not 1,800 of them. The tooltip reads the samples themselves, never the thinned line.
- **One pass per change.** A sample pushed, a series hidden, another size, theme or language: the samples in view are scanned once for the range, the points picked and the paths built again. A frame in which nothing of that changed, or only the pointer moved, scans nothing and picks nothing (`chart/tests/driven.rs` counts both, on every `cargo test`).
- **One clock per chart.** Every series of a chart is sampled at the same times: `push` takes one time and a value for each. That is what every React chart was given (the container's stats, and `chartData` for Pods and Nodes, each build one array of times), so series on clocks of their own are not supported: pushed as samples that each hold one series' value, they would be dots, and a counter's rate would be taken across the other's samples. A page with two streams makes two charts, or samples both on one clock.
- **A window's first line** comes in at the plot's edge where the last sample before the window and the first in it put it, worked out from the samples; that sample is neither pointed at nor counted in the range. **Day ticks** stand on each day's midnight on the chart's clock, across a change of the clocks.
- **A clock set back** stops the chart until it has caught up: a sample that does not move time forward is left out, so that one late sample costs nothing. A page that knows the clock changed calls `state.reset()`.
- **Labels are never cut.** A y axis that would take more than 45 % of the chart's width, and a time label that would stand on another, are left out whole; the tooltip says the values.
- **Nothing is clipped by the canvas.** The two renderers place a clipped frame differently, so the chart draws nothing that needs it: the one line that comes from before a window is cut at the plot's edge by hand.
- **The keyboard has no crosshair**: the tooltip is the pointer's. uPlot had none either.
- **One y axis, one unit** per chart; no zoom, no selection by dragging (React turned uPlot's off), no stacked areas.
- **Accessibility**: nothing is wired, as for the table (#228).

What a frame costs is held by `chart/tests/perf.rs` (`cargo test --release -p homeport-app --lib chart::tests::perf -- --ignored --nocapture --test-threads=1`), which fails when the chart's own share of a frame (`push`, `view`, layout, draw) goes over its budget; without `--release` it says so and gives eight times each. Ten series of 3,600 samples each, a 1100 × 420 window, 4 cores of a cloud VM, software renderer:

| What, with ten series of 3,600 samples                              | Budget | Release, two runs | Not optimised |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------ | ----------------- | ------------- |
| A frame that changes nothing                                        | 1 ms   | 0.06 ms           | 0.5 ms        |
| The pointer moving over the plot: the crosshair and its tooltip     | 1 ms   | 0.20 to 0.21 ms   | 1.9 ms        |
| Its worst frame                                                     | 8 ms   | 0.5 to 0.8 ms     | 3.5 ms        |
| A sample appended before every frame: range, points and paths again | 4 ms   | 1.0 ms            | 11 ms (of 32) |
| Its worst frame                                                     | 8 ms   | 1.4 to 2.2 ms     | 16 ms (of 64) |
| The same with 300 samples a series                                  |        | 0.27 ms           | 2.3 ms        |
| Appending one sample to the ten full rings                          |        | 0.003 ms          | 0.02 ms       |

A stream gives a sample a second, so the frame that costs a millisecond comes once a second and the others are still. **What is proven and what is not.** Held on every `cargo test`, in counts no machine changes (`chart/tests/driven.rs`): after a sample, ten series of 3,600 cost one pass over the samples and ten lines of no more points than the plot has pixels; between two samples, a frame works nothing out, pointer moving or not; gaps do not undo that. Timed, in the release profile, by a test that is ignored because a clock is not a thing to fail a build on: the table above, the chart's own work. Not proven: sixty frames a second on screen. The software renderer fills the window's pixels in 34 to 37 ms with these ten lines (13 ms with 300 samples a series), two frames of sixty a second for every frame that is drawn; the window draws with wgpu, which keeps the lines as a mesh between draws and which these tests cannot run, so what wgpu's dashed strokes cost when a sample comes is unmeasured. On a machine that falls back to the software renderer, a chart of ten long series is drawn at under thirty frames a second. The not-optimised column was measured at two points a pixel, before the thinning was set to one, and both columns before the buckets stood on time.

## The terminal

`widgets::terminal` is `XTerm` (`src/shared/ui/terminal`): a container's, a containerd task's, an Incus Instance's exec and console, a Pod's and a Nomad task's terminal. The emulation is `alacritty_terminal` 0.26 (Apache-2.0, pinned in the workspace): its grid, scrollback, alternate screen, parser (`vte`), selection and search. Its own pty and event loop are not used: a terminal of the app reads a session's stream, never a process of this computer. Look at it on the gallery's _Terminal_ page (a shell's output with every colour and attribute, wide text, emoji, box drawing and links, which echoes what is typed; a full-screen program's frame; `yes` and a rain of letters to pour in; the text's size); `terminal/mod.rs` has the example to copy.

- **The page keeps a `terminal::State`** per terminal, made once: `terminal::State::new()`, with `.with_scrollback(lines)` (10,000 unless told), `.with_font_size(px)` (12 unless told, 8 to 32; `set_font_size` changes it later), `.blinking(false)` for who asked for less motion, and `.dark(ctx.world.theme.is_dark())`, what a program that asks for the colours is told before the terminal is first drawn (the drawing tells it after): `ctx.world.theme` is the theme on screen, resolved by the window, the OS's when the Settings follow it. `terminal::Terminal::new(&state, Message::Terminal).toolbar(controls).failed(failure).height(..).view()` draws it: `toolbar` is what the page puts over the grid (the search bar goes after it while open), `failed` an `error_message` over the grid. It takes the room it is given and makes as many cells of it as fit (`addon-fit`); in a page that scrolls, give it a height.
- **`state.update(message)`** takes every `terminal::Message` and returns `(Task<Message>, Vec<terminal::Event>)`. Map the task (it gives the search field focus, and the grid back, and builds a regular expression on another thread) and act on each event, in order: `Event::Input(bytes)` goes to the session (`session.input(bytes)`, let go of quietly once the session ended or failed: the failure is what `session.written(..)` returns, shown once), `Event::Resize { cols, rows }` too (`session.resize(cols, rows)`), `Event::Copy(text)` is for `ctx.copy`, `Event::Open(url)` for `ctx.open_url` (web, mail and phone links only), `Event::Title` (256 characters at most) and `Event::Bell` are a program's, for a page that shows them.
- **What the session printed** goes to `state.feed(&output.text)`, a batch at a time; it returns the events the program's own queries produced (where the cursor is, which terminal this is, its colours, its size), which go to the session as any input. A megabyte is read at once and the rest waits for the next frames, which the grid asks for (`Message::Read`); no more than 8 MB waits: past it the oldest unread output is dropped, from a line's start (`State::dropped` counts it), as the stream's backlog drops the oldest when the window is behind, so after Ctrl + C the screen is never more than eight frames late. `state.notice(text)` writes a dimmed line of the page's own after the output: how the session ended, in the user's language (React wrote `\x1b[2m…\x1b[22m`); it first ends whatever the program left open (a string it was writing, a synchronised update, its colours: `CAN`, `SGR 0`), so nothing it left hides or colours the line. Behind output that waits to be read, the line waits in its place and is written in its turn, so a notice never reads the backlog at once. `state.reset()` before a new session: nothing of the last one is kept but the size, the scrollback's length and the search's query. A synchronised update (`?2026h`) is drawn whole when it ends, or as it is once its 150 ms are up, which the grid asks for when they are.
- **Drawing.** Rows are drawn on iced's advanced widget, text run by run: a row whose cells did not change since the last layout is taken as it was, wherever it moved, and shaped text is kept by what it says (`pane/rows.rs`), so only what a program changed is looked at again. The 16 colours are the theme's terminal palette, the 256 and 24-bit ones what they are, and text that would not read on what is behind it is moved until it does (the log viewer's `palette::colours`, the same rule). Bold is the bright form of the first eight (React's `fontWeightBold: 'normal'`: the monospaced face has one weight); italic, underline (every kind as a line), inverse, strike, dim and hidden are drawn. Characters the monospaced face lacks are each placed at their own cell, a wide one centred in its two; box drawing (U+2500 to U+257F but the diagonals) and block elements (U+2580 to U+259F) are drawn as shapes, so tmux's and htop's lines join from cell to cell at any size: rounded corners as arcs, dashed lines with their gaps, and double lines with their inner corners open where they meet (`╔ ╬ ╠`), as a font draws them. A row is built again when its cells, the colours a program set, the size of the text or the cells change. Combining marks stay with their letter.
- **The cursor** is a block, an underline or a bar as the program asks (`DECSCUSR`), blinks at xterm.js's pace (600 ms) while the grid has focus and is a hollow block without it. Its blink is the widget's own redraws: no message reaches the page for it.
- **The scrollback** scrolls with the wheel, the scrollbar (at the end of the row in the app's language: on the left in Arabic) and Shift with Page Up, Page Down, Home and End. A key typed or a paste brings the view back to the screen. On the alternate screen the wheel is the arrow keys (`ALTERNATE_SCROLL`), as in xterm.js.
- **The pointer** selects by cells (a drag; Shift and a press take it further), words (two presses: a word ends at a space, a tab, a comma, a colon, a pipe, a quote, a backtick, a bracket or a box line, `alacritty_terminal`'s list, so a path is one) and lines (three), across the scrollback; the selection moves with its text when new lines come. A program that asked for the pointer (`?1000h` and its kin) is told of presses, releases, moves and the wheel instead, in SGR's encoding, UTF-8's or xterm's first one (`mouse.rs`); Shift with a press still selects, and a release is told whatever is held, so a drag the program follows always ends. A link, written (`http`, `https`, `mailto`) or named by the program (OSC 8), is underlined under the pointer and opened with Ctrl, or Cmd on macOS, and a press.
- **Pasting** sends the clipboard's text with its line ends as Enter (xterm.js's `prepareTextForTerminal`), between the brackets of a bracketed paste when the program asked (`?2004h`), with every Escape taken out (as alacritty does), so nothing pasted can close the brackets early however it is nested. A program never reads or writes the clipboard (OSC 52 is off).
- **Searching** (Ctrl + Shift + F, Cmd + F) opens a bar over the grid: the log viewer's field, count and toggles (`Aa`, `.*`), the steps, a copy of the selection, and a button that closes it. It looks through the scrollback and the screen with `alacritty_terminal`'s own search, which finds a match across the rows a long line wrapped onto. It lists the matches of the 1,000 lines above the view and below it (`search::NEAR`), up to 10,000, starts on the latest and brings the one it stands on into view; the count has a "+" when lines further off were not looked at (a list of a whole scrollback of 10,000 lines of 120 columns took 18 to 28 ms optimised, most of a frame), and says "No matches nearby" when none is near. Next and previous go on past the lines listed, through the whole scrollback and round its ends, and the list follows the view (scrolled, or brought to a match). Enter in the field goes to the next match, Shift + Enter to the one before, Escape closes the bar and gives the grid the keyboard. What is selected when it opens is what it looks for; opened again while open, its field takes the keyboard and the search goes on where it stood. Plain text of up to 256 bytes is made into its matcher where it is typed; a regular expression is built on another thread, as in the log viewer. While the bar is open the matches are listed again when output comes, at most every 250 ms while it pours (all of them near the view: `alacritty_terminal` keeps no count of the lines it let go of once its scrollback is full, so the new lines alone can't be read), and the one the search stands on stays itself: followed up by the lines that came under it while the scrollback is not full, and once it is, found again by its place in its row and what its row says (among rows that all say the same, the one now where it stood). Closing the bar lets go of the matches and their marks; the query stays for the next time.
- **The input method**: the grid asks for one while it has focus (`Purpose::Terminal`), shows what is being composed over the spot (iced draws the preedit beside the cursor) and sends what is committed. No input method could be run here: that composing works with a real one (Japanese, Chinese, Korean) is unchecked, an open item for the platform checks (#284).
- **In Arabic** the bar and the scrollbar mirror; the grid reads left to right and the keys are not mirrored.
- **Accessibility**: nothing is wired, as for the table (#228); each row says its text to an operation, which the tests read.

The keyboard, while the grid has focus (`terminal/keys.rs`). Every key a program can be sent is sent as xterm sends it: the cursor keys (`SS3` in the application cursor mode, `CSI 1 ; m` with modifiers), Home, End, Insert, Delete, Page Up and Down (`CSI n ~`), F1 to F20, Enter (CR, or CR LF in new-line mode), Tab and Shift + Tab (`CSI Z`), Backspace (DEL; Ctrl: BS), Escape, Ctrl with a letter or `@ [ \ ] ^ _ ?` (their control characters), Alt as an Escape before the key (on macOS Option types what the layout makes of it), the keypad's own codes when the program asks (`DECKPAM`). Ctrl and the terminal's own letters go by where the key is on a US layout when the layout's letter there is not Latin (Ctrl + С on a Russian layout is ^C, Ctrl + Shift + С copies); Ctrl and Alt together that type text (AltGr on Windows: `@` on a German layout) send the text; Ctrl with a sign that has no control character (Ctrl + 1, Ctrl + ,) sends the sign, as xterm does. The terminal's own keys and those it leaves alone:

| Key                                        | What it does                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Ctrl + Shift + C (Cmd + C on macOS)        | copies the selection                                               |
| Ctrl + Shift + V, Shift + Insert (Cmd + V) | pastes                                                             |
| Ctrl + Shift + F (Cmd + F)                 | opens the search bar, or goes to its field                         |
| Ctrl + Shift + A (Cmd + A)                 | selects everything the terminal holds                              |
| Shift + Page Up, Page Down                 | a screen of the scrollback (the program's on the alternate screen) |
| Shift + Home, End                          | the oldest line kept, the screen (the same)                        |
| Ctrl + Tab, Ctrl + Shift + Tab             | not the terminal's: focus leaves it, forward and back              |
| Cmd with any other key (macOS)             | not the terminal's: the app's shortcuts fire                       |
| any other Ctrl or Alt combination          | the program's (what xterm sends, or nothing): never the app's      |
| any other key                              | the program's                                                      |

**The app's shortcuts never take a key the terminal used.** The shell's key area hears such a key as the terminal's (`shortcuts::keys::Target::Terminal`, where nothing fires), so Ctrl + K kills to the end of the line instead of opening the palette, Ctrl + 1 to Ctrl + 9 and Ctrl + , go to the program instead of changing page, and `/`, `n` and `?` are typed. On macOS the shortcuts are Cmd's, which the terminal leaves alone, so they all fire from a terminal; elsewhere Mod is Ctrl, which programs need, and every Ctrl or Alt combination is the terminal's, so none does, as in React, where nothing fired from xterm.js. Tab is the shell's completion: Ctrl + Tab leaves the terminal. A widget that needs every key reports itself with `focus::EveryKey`; `focus::key_area(..).on_terminal(..)` is the handler for what it used.

**A session** is `data::streams::session` (`docs/agents/iced-migration.md`, _Reading data and following streams_): a `TerminalSession`, made with the backend and the terminal's size (`TerminalSession::new(backend, state.size())`), follows the output (`session.subscription(ctx.state)`, opened at that size; what it gives goes through `session.take`) and sends the input and the sizes, the size first whenever a session starts. Its module has a page's whole code for a terminal, which its tests run. The gallery has no session: it echoes what is typed.

Its limits:

- **Memory** is `alacritty_terminal`'s: 24 bytes a cell, so 10,000 lines of 200 columns are 48 MB; the scrollback's length is the page's to choose.
- **An OSC string a program never ends** (`ESC ]` with no `BEL` or `ST`) is kept whole by the parser until it ends or another sequence starts: `vte` 0.15 caps it at 1,024 bytes only when built without `std`, and `alacritty_terminal` 0.26 turns `std` on with no way to turn it off. Upstream, not ours to cap; the app's own line after a session (`state.notice`) ends such a string (`CAN`), and so does the next `ESC`, but the room it took is kept until `state.reset()` makes a new parser.
- **A link** is found on one row: a link a program wrapped onto the next row is opened up to the row's end (OSC 8 links have no such limit). A regular expression of the search does not anchor `^` and `$` to lines.
- **xterm's first pointer encoding** stops at cell 95, not 223: a value over 127 is a byte xterm sends raw, and input is a `String` through the session's API (`terminal::Event::Input`, `TerminalSession::input` and the five `*_input` use cases; the runtimes' writers under them take bytes), whose bytes over 127 come in pairs and would name another cell. Programs that want far cells ask for SGR (`?1006h`), as vim, tmux and htop do.
- **Colours** are worked out once a look and kept, 4,096 looks at most (a program of 24-bit colours has as many as it likes).
- **Glyphs of the system's fonts** (CJK, emoji, other scripts) are placed at their cells but drawn as the font draws them: an emoji the font has no colour for is drawn in the text's colour.
- **Not done**: the kitty keyboard protocol, a block selection (Alt and a drag), vi mode, the primary selection of X11 (a middle press pastes nothing), images (sixel), double-width and double-height lines (`DECDWL`, which `alacritty_terminal` ignores too).

What a frame costs under a flood is measured by `terminal/tests/perf.rs` (`cargo test --release -p homeport-app --lib terminal::tests::perf -- --ignored --nocapture --test-threads=1`): 50 MB of `yes` fed 160 KiB a frame (ten megabytes a second at sixty frames), and a rain of letters that writes every cell again each frame, with a key typed and handled in each frame. It fails when the terminal's own share of a frame goes over 33 ms (thirty a second), eight times that in a build that is not optimised. On 4 cores of a cloud VM, a 1100 × 720 window (a grid of 138 × 40 cells), the software renderer, the release profile, one run:

| What, a frame of each                             | Read   | Layout | Draw    | The terminal's own (worst) | Pixels  | Frames a second, pixels included |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------ | ------ | ------- | -------------------------- | ------- | -------------------------------- |
| `yes`, 160 KiB a frame, 50 MB in all (320 frames) | 1.5 ms | 0.2 ms | 0.01 ms | 1.7 ms (25.6 ms)           | 4.5 ms  | 140                              |
| A rain of letters, all 5,520 cells written again  | 0.7 ms | 3.4 ms | 0.25 ms | 4.4 ms (12.9 ms)           | 21.6 ms | 37                               |
| The same on 80 × 24                               | 0.2 ms | 1.3 ms | 0.06 ms | 1.6 ms (7.9 ms)            | 10.1 ms | 81                               |

A key typed during each of those frames was handled in 0.001 ms of `update`: batches come at most sixty times a second, so a key waits for one frame at most. The 50 MB of `yes` were drawn in 2.3 s with every frame drawn (22 MB a second); read without drawing, the parser and the grid take 10 ms a megabyte (97 MB a second), which is why a batch is read a megabyte at a time (`READ_AT_ONCE`): a window that fell behind gets up to 16 MB in one batch, read over the next frames, each drawn. Not optimised, the same frames take 21 ms (`yes`) and 32 ms (rain) of the terminal's own, and the software renderer's pixels 93 and 319 ms: a debug build keeps up with neither. The window draws with wgpu, which was not measured; the rain's pixels are many short pieces of text, one a cell whose colour changes, which is the software renderer's slowest case. What is not proven: thirty frames a second on screen with wgpu, and the worst frame of `yes` (25.6 ms, the scrollback's first growth) recurring.

## The data widgets

`widgets::data` and a few small parts beside it: look at them on the gallery's _Data_ page.

- **The file browser** (`data::file_browser`): a folder's listing under the path that leads to it. The page keeps a `file_browser::State` (its table's sort and scroll), reads the folder itself and gives the entries: `FileBrowser::new(&state, &path, entries, Message::Files).failed(error).uploads(true).view()`, where `entries` is `None` while the folder is read (skeletons show) and `failed` is the `AppError` of a folder that could not be (said in place of the rows, with Details and a Retry). `state.update(message, entries)` gives back what the page is asked to do: `Event::Navigate(path)` (a press or Enter on a folder, a press on a part of the path), `Event::Refresh` (the button, or Retry), `Event::Download(entry)` (the entry's menu: the `…` button, the secondary button, the menu key), `Event::Upload` (the button `uploads(true)` shows) and `Event::Failure(event)`. It opens no dialog and moves no file: those are the page's. On `Event::Upload` the page asks `ctx.platform.pick_files(OpenFile::new(t!("containers.files.uploadTo", ..)))`, on `Event::Download(entry)` `ctx.platform.save_file(..)` under the entry's name made safe (`gallery::data::save_request`: `name.tar` for a folder), and a cancelled dialog does nothing; `gallery/data.rs` is the example. The transfer itself, into or out of the container, is the Engine's use case, started with `ctx.act` once the path is known: the gallery only says what it would ask, and the container's Files tab (`ui/features/docker/containers/detail/tabs/files.rs`) does it, a file at a time, with a count and a Cancel. `file_browser::Entry` is `FileEntry` without the Engine's crate; a page maps one to the other. `.actions(vec![..])` adds the page's own buttons after Refresh. Columns sort (name as `sort::natural`, size, date, mode), resize and hide as any table's; the order the page gives is the order shown until a header is pressed. `crumbs` is `fileBrowser.model.ts`.
- **The JSON view** (`data::json`): a document as a tree in the kit's table, a name and a value a row. The page reads the document once, `json::Document::parse(text)` or `Document::of(&value)` (a pass over all of it: in a task when it may be megabytes), and keeps `json::State::new(document)`; `json::View::new(&state, Message::Json).max_height(..).view()` draws it and `state.update(message)` returns a task to map and `Some(json::Event::Copy(text))` for `ctx.copy`. A press or Enter on an object or an array closes and opens it; a row's menu copies its path as `jq` writes it (`.Config.Env[0]`, `.Labels["com.docker.compose.project"]`) or its value on indented lines; the Copy button copies the whole document, as React's did. The search field is `state.search_id()` for `/`; Enter there goes to the next match and Shift + Enter to the one before, round at the ends, opening what holds the match and scrolling to it. It looks in members' names and in values as JSON writes them, whatever the case.
- **Labelled values** (`data::key_value(vec![Item::text(label, value), Item::mono(..), Item::new(label, element), Item::maybe(label, option)])`): the labels in a column as wide as the longest (240 px at most), the values in the rest, wrapped anywhere, a dash for a value there is none of.
- **The search field** (`form::search`) has a button at its end that empties it once it holds something, and Escape empties it and stays in it; on an empty field Escape leaves it. What is typed reaches the page at once. `form::Debounce` keeps the text and tells, 250 ms after the last letter, whether nothing was typed since: `self.search.input(text).map(Message::Paused)`, then `self.search.settled(ticket)` is the query to run.
- **A field with suggestions** (`form::suggest`) is the browser's `<input list>` over a `<datalist>`: what is typed stays free text, and a list under the field offers the suggestions that hold it, whatever the case (Chromium's and WebKit's filter), but the one typed whole; every one over an empty field. The page keeps a `suggest::State` and the suggestions (`&[String]`, built once, not in `view`): `suggest(&state, &suggestions, &value).placeholder(..).font(..).view()`, and `state.update(message, &value, &suggestions)` returns a task (focus that leaves on Tab) and `Some(Event::Input(text))` for what the field holds now or `Some(Event::Submit)` for Enter with nothing highlighted (the form's Enter). The list opens as the text changes or on Down; Down and Up highlight (round at the ends) while the keyboard stays in the field; Enter takes the highlighted suggestion, Escape closes the list and only the list (a dialog around stays), Tab and Shift + Tab close it and leave, a press takes one and a press elsewhere closes it. It hangs from the field's start: the right edge in Arabic. The gallery's _Controls_ page has one (`widgets-suggest-*`).
- **A copy button's feedback**: `feedback::Copied<T>` says which button of several copied (`copied.is(&what)`), and a button pressed again goes on saying "Copied" until its own second and a half is over: `copied.start(what).map(Message::CopyShown)`, then `copied.finish(ticket)`.

Their limits:

- **A large JSON document** is virtualised, not bounded: the rows built are those in view (a few dozen cells for 800,001 nodes as for 8,001, counted by `data/tests/large.rs`), and the rows that show are worked out once when a node opens or closes, a pass over them. A document of more than 2,000 nodes starts with only its root open. The search reads 20,000 nodes at an `update` and asks for the next slice by a message, so the window draws in between; each slice lowers the case of what it reads, so a value of megabytes is one long step. A value is shown up to 400 characters (cut between characters), whole on the clipboard. Memory is the document's text again, and about 90 bytes a node.
- **The JSON tree's rows are the table's**: one Tab stop, the arrows up and down, Enter to open and close; Left and Right scroll the columns and do not close a node. A search run before the view was ever drawn scrolls by the room the table assumes, and may leave the match under the fold.
- **A JSON document is read by the view's own reader** (`json/document.rs`), not into a `serde_json::Value`: a number is kept, shown and copied digit for digit as the document wrote it (`1e400`, `-0`, an integer of thirty digits), a name written twice is there twice (with the same path), and nothing recurses, so a document nests as deep as its text goes (200,000 levels are tested; rows are set in for the first 24). `Document::parse` says where a text stops being JSON (`Invalid { offset, why }`), for the page to make an `AppError` of. `serde_json`'s `arbitrary_precision` would have kept the numbers too, but it is a feature of the whole workspace's `serde_json` and changes what every crate's `Value` holds; nothing else was touched.
- **What a string says is what is searched**, as it was typed, spaces included: `C:\\Users` in the document is found by `C:\Users`, and a quote finds the values that hold one, not every string. A character that draws nothing or turns the text around it (a control character, U+202E, a joiner of no width) is shown as its escape, so one value cannot be made to read as another; the clipboard gets the document's own text.
- **A document read again** is given with `state.set_document(document)` (map the task): what was closed and opened stays, by path, where the new document has it; the table keeps its scroll; the search runs again. Copying writes the text out in a task, so `Event::Copy` comes from a later `update` than the press.
- **The file browser is a list, not a tree**: one folder at a time, as React's. A link is not followed by a press. The path is not editable.
- **The shortcuts' Mac signs** ⌘ ⌥ ⇧ ↵ are in none of the app's fonts (their character maps were read: Host Grotesk and Fragment Mono have the four arrows, the ellipsis and the dash, and none of those four), so they are drawn by whatever font the OS falls back to. A Mac has them; that they show there is unchecked and is an open item for the macOS work (#236, #284). The snapshots of this page show them because the machine that drew them had a font with them.
- **Accessibility**: nothing is wired, as for the table (#228).

## Rules for a page

- **A line that starts with a sign.** A line takes its direction from its first letter; one that starts with a dot, a number or a `technical` string has none and reads left to right, so `· 3 عمليات` after a title would put its dot at the far end. `basic::in_reading_order(text)` makes it read the way the language does.
- **Rows.** Write a row start to end with `widgets::line` and columns with `widgets::stack`; align with `start()` and `end()`. The page then mirrors in Arabic by itself. For text that fills a row, use `widgets::filling(text)`, and `widgets::paragraph` for body text: a `text` told to fill its width _and_ align right is drawn out of place in a right-to-left script.
- **A row that must keep its last item.** A `line` is as wide as what it holds, and then gives a filling item all the room before the items after it: a badge after a long text is pushed out of its cell. Tell the row to fill (`line(..).width(Length::Fill)`) and cut the text (`filling(text).clip(true)`): the badge keeps its place (a container's status and its health, an image and its "newer" mark).
- **Buttons.** Use the kit's (`basic::button`, or `focus::pressable` for an odd one), never iced's `button`: iced's takes no keyboard focus.
- **Tab.** The window sends `focus::traverse(shift)` for a Tab key press nothing captured (`focus::tab_presses()`). A dialog or an open panel captures Tab and keeps focus inside.
- **Overlays.** Their state is the page's (`menu::State`, `select::State`, a `bool` for a popover, an `Option` for a dialog), fed by their messages. What opened one keeps its focus underneath, so focus is back there when it closes. A page returns its dialog from its `dialog` function and the shell lays it over the window (`docs/agents/iced-migration.md`); what hosts a whole window lays a dialog with `overlay::over(page, dialog)` and toasts with `toast::over(.., toasts)`, even when there is none: they build the same tree either way, and iced keeps a widget's state (what is scrolled, what has focus) only while the tree around it keeps its shape. Toasts go over the dialog (`toast::over(overlay::over(page, dialog), toasts)`), so a failure is said while the dialog is up.
- **Focus.** A ring shows for focus the keyboard gave (Tab, an arrow, a dialog opened with a key), not for a click: `focus::modality` keeps who was last there. One ring shows at a time: a button's hides while the menu or the list it opened is up. A button inside a field (`button::keep_focus`) leaves focus in the field. A menu and a select are modal: a press outside closes them and presses nothing; a popover lets the press through. A dialog that opens gives focus to its first widget in reading order (the topmost row's start: the right in Arabic, though a mirrored row is laid out from the left), or to the one it names with `modal(..).focus_first(id)`; every confirmation names its Cancel, as Radix's `AlertDialog` did, so Enter never confirms what can't be undone.
- **The window** (`ui/mod.rs`) hosts the one `Toasts` and the one Details dialog, and takes `Message::ThemeChanged` and `Message::LanguageChanged` when the Settings change.
- **Failures.** Make a `feedback::Surfaced::new(error, "use_case_name")` where the failure reaches the page: that writes the log line. Show it with `error_message` in place or `Toasts::failure` in passing; both lead to the Details dialog. State keeps the `AppError`, never its text.
- **Forms.** A use case's refusal goes through `form::Errors::route(error, &[(key, field)])`: under its field when its key is about one, else back to the form for `error_message`.

## What differs from the React app, and why

- The focus ring is drawn just outside a button, as CSS did; a field shows focus as its border (iced draws a field inside its bounds).
- A dialog's scrim dims more (25 %) and doesn't blur: iced has no blur.
- `EmptyState`'s edge is a whole hairline: iced draws no dashed border.
- `Skeleton` and a connecting `StatusDot` don't pulse; the spinner turns.
- A tooltip waits 300 ms under the pointer (the React one showed at once) and shows at once on keyboard focus. A press or Escape sends it away; focus given by a click doesn't show it.
- A submenu that had to open on the other side keeps its chevron pointing the way the language reads.
- Every tab of a `tabs` row is a Tab stop; Radix made the chosen one the only stop.
- In a right-to-left row, Tab goes through the buttons left to right, the order they are laid out in. Arrows, Home and End follow the language.
- A plain `select` doesn't scroll its list to the option the arrows reach; a list long enough to scroll should be `searchable`, whose `State::reveal` does.
- iced's `checkbox` and `toggler` keep their box before the label in Arabic.
- An empty state's description is centred line by line left to right; right to left its block is centred and its lines start on the right.
- A select's button keeps one line and cuts a label too long for it.
- The table. React's `DataTable` cut long lists into pages (`pageSize`) and only the Board drew a virtual list; here every table is virtual and none has pages (`ui.table.page`, `previous` and `next` are unused).
- The rows of a table are one Tab stop, moved in with the arrows; in React each row's first cell was a button and a stop, and so was the chevron of a Stack's header. Space selects the row the keyboard is on (in React it pressed that button, which opened the row); Enter opens it.
- A press on a row selects it and a double press opens it, unless the page asks for `opening_on_press`, which is what React did everywhere. Ranges with Shift, Ctrl or Cmd + A, Escape, the keyboard on rows, typing to a row, resizable and hideable columns and the menu on the secondary button are new.
- The checkbox over the rows, when only some are selected, is the React one as it was drawn: an empty box with the mark, not a filled one with a dash. A group's checkbox shows the same mark when only some of its rows are selected; React's was either on or off.
- A table's scrollbar is its own (it scrolls rows it has not built): its thumb is darker than a scroll area's `--border`, which could not be seen on a selected row, and it is on the left in Arabic. It has no kinetic or touch scrolling.
- The filter is given the fields to look in; React's looked in every column that had a value.
- No expandable rows (no React table had any), no columns to move, no row heights measured from their content (one height per table, `State::row_height`), no pinned column when the columns scroll sideways.
- The log viewer is a list of lines, not a terminal (React drew logs in a read-only xterm.js): sequences that move the cursor or clear the screen are taken out rather than obeyed, and a carriage return only starts its line over.
- The log view pauses by itself when scrolled up and follows again at the end; in React it followed until the Follow toggle was turned off, and scrolling up while lines came was undone by the next line. "Jump to latest" is new.
- The log's Timestamps toggle hides and shows what the lines carry; React opened the stream again each time (a page can still do that: `Event::Timestamps`). Only what starts with a digit before the first space is a timestamp; React took whatever stood there, the first word of a line without one included.
- The log's search is done as it is typed, counts its matches, and has the case and regular expression toggles and the filter; React searched on Enter, whatever the case, as text. The match the search is on is amber seen through in the dark theme, so the light text on it reads; xterm drew a solid amber.
- A program's colours are moved until they read on the viewer (3:1); xterm drew them as asked, black on the dark theme's black included. A line of stderr is tinted and marked; React drew both streams alike.
- The labels of a multi-source log are padded to the longest label there is now; React padded each line to the longest known when it was written. Each source has its own colour state; React's sources shared one terminal's.
- A log's wrap toggle, its text selection across the scrollback with Ctrl or Cmd + A and C, the lines skipped said between the lines, and a cap the page gives are new: xterm always wrapped, kept 50,000 rows, and the viewer kept 100,000 lines to save. Text is placed by cells counted from Unicode's tables rather than by a terminal's own; glyphs of scripts the monospaced face lacks may not fill their cells.
- The chart's values under the pointer are in a tooltip beside the crosshair; uPlot wrote them in the legend, after each series' name and after "Time". The legend is at the start of its line, not centred, and its entries are buttons the keyboard reaches; uPlot's were pressable with the pointer only.
- The chart's y axis is on the right in Arabic and its legend mirrors; React's stayed on the left in every language. Time runs left to right in both.
- The chart's ticks for sizes stand on powers of two of their unit (128 MB); uPlot stepped by tens of bytes, which read as 95.4 MB. A y axis is as wide as its labels; React's was 70 px whatever they said.
- A counter's rate is taken by the chart (`State::counter`), where React's page computed it (`rates`) before giving the chart its data: same rule, a gap at the first sample and after a reset. A value that is `NaN` or infinite is a gap; uPlot was given what the page gave it. A chart without samples draws its baseline and no axis. A sample that comes late is left out and the rest kept, as `rates` did; a counter that missed a reading takes its next rate across the gap, where React had no missing reading to deal with.
- Text a machine named (a file, a path, a JSON key or value, a digest) is laid out left to right whatever its letters, with `basic::technical`: a file named in Arabic keeps its extension at its end in both directions. React left it to the browser, which starts a paragraph from its first letter. Container names and image references need none: the Engine allows them ASCII only.
- A search field in a dialog or a panel empties on the first Escape and the dialog closes on the next; what is inside a dialog now sees Escape before the dialog does.
- A chart of more than two or three series has colours of its own (a blue and an olive beside ink and grey) and five dash patterns; React had `--chart-1` to `-5`, ink and grey, and one dash. The pages that port a React chart keep its ink and grey.
- Long series are thinned to a point a pixel, a window of time can be asked for, and a line can stop where the stream did; uPlot drew every point and joined across.
- The JSON view is a tree with a search and a menu on each row; React printed the document as text with one Copy button, in a box that scrolled. The members of an object keep the order the document has them in, as there. Text can no longer be selected with the pointer: a row's menu copies its value.
- The file browser's download is in each entry's menu (the `…` at the row's end, the secondary button, the menu key); React had a download button in every row. While a folder is read its rows are skeletons; React wrote "Loading…". Its path reads left to right in every language; React's row of parts mirrored in Arabic. Upload is the browser's own button when the page says files can be put there; React's page gave the button as `actions`, which it still can.
- A search field has a button that empties it, and Escape empties it before it leaves it; React's field had neither, and its Escape left an empty field only. A debounce is there for the searches that cost; React had none.
- A copy button pressed twice says "Copied" for a second and a half after the last press; React's first timer ended it.
- `KeyValue`'s labels are capped at 240 px and wrap past it; React's column was as wide as the longest label, however long.
- The lines a log saves have no escape sequences; React saved the text as the program wrote it, colours' sequences included.
- A log stream that breaks is said over the lines with `error_message` (`Viewer::failed`), with its Details; React wrote the message in red among the lines.
- The terminal is `alacritty_terminal`'s emulation, not xterm.js's. What differs, on purpose: the scrollback keeps 10,000 lines unless the page asks (xterm.js was given 50,000: a cell takes twice the memory here); text that would not read on what is behind it is moved until it does, as in the log viewer (xterm.js drew a program's colours as asked); box drawing and blocks are drawn as shapes, not by a font; links are opened with Ctrl or Cmd and a press (React's terminals had no links); the search counts its matches and has the case and regular expression toggles (React had `addon-search` loaded and no bar); Ctrl + Shift + C and V, Cmd + C and V, copy and paste (xterm.js had the browser's menu and Ctrl + Shift + V of the browser); the cursor blinks at 600 ms on a widget's own redraws; Ctrl + Tab leaves the terminal, where nothing let focus out of xterm.js but the pointer. A program never reads or sets the clipboard (OSC 52), and its queries of the colours are answered with the theme's. A bracketed paste has every Escape taken out (xterm.js only removed the closing sequence when asked; alacritty does this).
